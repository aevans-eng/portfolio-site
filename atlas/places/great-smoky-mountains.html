The hook

The most visited national park in the United States, by a wide margin, and one of the most biologically diverse temperate places on Earth — some 19,000 documented species, with tens of thousands more thought to be undescribed, including a global capital of salamanders.

01Why it's worth it

The Smokies are old mountains, worn down and forested to the top, and the appeal is depth rather than drama: mist rising off ridge after ridge, coves of old-growth hardwood, more than 1,400 km of trail including a long stretch of the Appalachian Trail, and preserved Appalachian homesteads at Cades Cove and Cataloochee where elk have been reintroduced. Because of the elevation range and the fact that glaciers never reached here, the biodiversity is extraordinary — this is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Biosphere Reserve on those grounds. In early June, one of the few synchronous firefly populations on Earth flashes in unison here, by lottery-access only. And uniquely among the great American parks, entry is free, a condition of the land transfer that created it.

The honest side: that combination of free entry and East Coast population makes it crowded, and it draws a fair "overrated" vote from park regulars — one names it alongside Joshua Tree as their most overrated. Gatlinburg at the gate is a strip of go-karts and pancake houses. The park rewards leaving the road.

02How to experience it

Drive Cades Cove at dawn on a weekday (it jams solid later) or cycle it on a vehicle-free morning. Walk to Andrews Bald or up Alum Cave to LeConte for the ridge views, and use Cataloochee for elk with a fraction of the people. Enter the firefly lottery in spring if the dates suit. October colour is spectacular and the traffic is genuinely bad; late April wildflowers are the quieter equal.

From the locals

It divides park regulars honestly. In an "overrated" thread it gets named directly — "most overrated for me would be Joshua Tree and Great Smoky Mountains" — while others list it among the eastern parks that shaped them before they saw the West. Everyone agrees on one practical point: it costs nothing to enter, which is rare enough to be worth stating.
